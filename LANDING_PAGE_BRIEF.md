# Landing Page Design Brief — Canja (Kenya-first invoicing & payments)

> **How to use this file:** paste it into your design tool as-is. It is written so a
> designer with zero prior context can produce something on-strategy. Each section
> explains the *why* behind the choice — a landing page is a conversion machine
> first and a pretty picture second.

---

## 1. The product, in one page

**What it is:** Canja is an invoicing and payments platform built for Kenyan
freelancers and small teams. You create professional, branded invoices and quotes,
get paid the way your clients actually pay (M-Pesa first), and keep a trustworthy
record of every shilling — who owes you, what's overdue, and what's been paid.

**Who it's for:** Kenyan freelancers, solo consultants, creatives, and small
agencies who today juggle Word/Excel templates, exported PDFs, and manual
WhatsApp/SMS follow-ups — with M-Pesa payments that never link back to an invoice.

**The core problem it kills:** No professional look, no clear view of who owes
money, no reliable record, and endless manual chasing. Cash flow is invisible and
disputes are avoidable-but-common.

**Why it wins (the wedge):** It's built around **M-Pesa** — the dominant way
Kenyans pay. Global tools (Zoho, QuickBooks, FreshBooks, Wave) assume cards and
Stripe, which isn't native for Kenyan merchants, forcing clunky workarounds. Canja
is local by design: KES, M-Pesa, KRA PIN, Kenyan business context.

**What it does (proof of substance):** branded invoices with multiple PDF templates
· quotes/estimates a client can view, accept, or decline online · automatic
conversion of accepted quotes to invoices · payment recording & reminders · credit
notes · a dashboard with cash-flow, receivables aging, and who-owes-you breakdowns ·
multi-currency · a full audit trail (issued documents are immutable). It is
production-grade, not a toy.

**Pricing model:** Freemium. The **core invoicing loop is free forever** (one user,
generous volume, full audit trail, manual payment recording) — the competitive
anchor is Zoho Invoice, so the free tier must feel genuinely complete. **Pro** adds
team seats, recurring invoices, automated reminders, multi-currency, custom
branding/templates, advanced reports, and a branded client portal.

## 2. Positioning & the one promise

Every high-converting page sells **one** promise, then proves it. Ours:

> **"From finished work to paid — in minutes, the M-Pesa way."**

The emotional job: make a freelancer feel *professional, in control of their money,
and done chasing.* The rational job: show it's faster than spreadsheets, built for
how Kenya pays, and free to start.

Headline candidates (pick one, A/B the rest):

- **"Invoices that actually get paid."**
  (sub: "Professional invoices, M-Pesa payments, and one clear record of every
  shilling — built for Kenya.")
- **"Bill it. Send it. Get paid."**
- **"Canja: invoicing built for how Kenya gets paid."**

## 3. Conversion strategy (read this before designing)

- **One primary CTA, everywhere:** *"Start free"* (freemium, **no card required** —
  say it out loud, it removes the biggest hesitation). A quiet secondary:
  *"See how it works"* (scrolls to the product demo).
- **Four psychological levers**, in priority order: **① Local trust** (M-Pesa, KES,
  "built for Kenya") → ② **Speed** ("minutes, not spreadsheets") → ③ **Money
  clarity** ("know exactly who owes you") → ④ **Risk reversal** ("free forever core,
  no card").
- **Trust is the product** — this handles people's money. Signal legitimacy above
  the fold and near every CTA: audit trail, secure, "your data is yours."
- **Mobile-first is non-negotiable.** Your audience lives on phones and variable
  connectivity. Design the mobile view *first*, keep it fast and light, and make
  CTAs thumb-reachable.
- **Show the product, don't describe it.** Real UI screenshots (invoice builder, a
  branded PDF, the dashboard, a phone showing the hosted invoice + M-Pesa) out-
  convert abstract illustration every time.

## 4. Page anatomy (section by section, with intent)

1. **Sticky slim nav** — logo left; "Features · Pricing · FAQ" center; persistent
   **"Start free"** button right. On scroll it condenses. This CTA follows the user
   down the whole page.
2. **Hero** — big confident headline + one-line subhead + primary/secondary CTA,
   and **a real product visual** (a browser frame showing a branded invoice or the
   dashboard, with a phone mockup overlapping it showing the hosted invoice + an
   M-Pesa payment prompt). Add a one-line trust cue under the buttons:
   *"Free forever core · No card · Built for Kenya."*
3. **Social-proof strip** — a slim band directly under the hero: "Trusted by
   freelancers and small teams across Kenya," a few logos or avatars, or a stat row
   (*"Invoices sent," "Paid via M-Pesa," "Hours saved"* — animated count-up on
   scroll). If you don't have real numbers yet, use outcome statements, never fake
   logos.
4. **Problem → agitation** — mirror their current pain in their words: "Word
   templates. Screenshot receipts. 'Have you paid yet?' on WhatsApp. No idea who
   still owes you." One tight section that makes them nod.
5. **Solution / How it works** — three steps with mini-visuals: **Create** (branded
   invoice/quote in minutes) → **Send** (email + a link the client opens, views, and
   pays) → **Get paid & tracked** (record the M-Pesa payment, status updates,
   dashboard reflects it). This is the "aha."
6. **Feature bento grid** — a modern bento layout of the substance: *Quotes clients
   accept online · Payment reminders on autopilot · Cash-flow & aging dashboard ·
   Multiple PDF templates · Multi-currency · Full audit trail.* Each cell = benefit
   headline + one line + a tiny product crop.
7. **Differentiation vs. the incumbents** — a confident, non-defensive comparison:
   "Zoho and QuickBooks are built for cards. Canja is built for M-Pesa." A small
   table or two-column contrast (Global tools vs. Canja) on local payments,
   KES/KRA, mobile.
8. **Trust & security** — audit trail, immutable records, "your data is yours,"
   reliable delivery. For a financial tool this earns the signup.
9. **Testimonials** — real quotes from Kenyan users if available (name, role, town).
   Authenticity > polish. Skip if you have none yet — never fabricate.
10. **Pricing** — two clean cards: **Free** (emphasize it's genuinely full-featured)
    and **Pro** (seats, recurring, reminders, multi-currency, branding, reports).
    Highlight Pro, but let Free feel like a real home, not a trap. Reinforce "no card
    to start."
11. **FAQ** — kill the last objections: *Is it really free? · How do M-Pesa payments
    work? · Can my clients pay online? · Is my data safe? · Do I need to be
    tech-savvy?*
12. **Final CTA band** — full-width, high-contrast (this is a great place for the one
    dark, accent-green section): "Send your first invoice in the next five minutes."
    One button: **Start free.**
13. **Footer** — links, contact, socials, "Made in Kenya 🇰🇪," legal.

## 5. Visual & brand direction

- **Color:** anchor on the brand **deep green `#103B05`** (confident, trustworthy,
  subtly "money/growth," and distinctly not the generic SaaS-purple everyone uses).
  Pair with a warm off-white canvas, near-black ink for text, and one bright accent
  for CTAs (a vivid green or a warm contrast like amber for buttons so they pop
  against the deep green). Use a soft green→teal gradient *sparingly* for one hero
  glow or the final CTA band — not everywhere.
- **Typography:** a modern, slightly expressive sans for display (large, tight
  tracking, confident weight) paired with a highly legible sans for body. Big type
  carries the hero; generous line-height keeps it human.
- **Imagery:** real product UI (crisp screenshots in clean browser/phone frames),
  Kenyan context where people appear, and an unmistakable M-Pesa payment moment.
  Prefer authentic product/photography over stock illustration.
- **Iconography:** simple line icons (lucide-style, matching the app) — consistent
  stroke, never decorative clutter.

## 6. Current design trends to apply (2026-modern, tastefully)

- **Bento-box grids** for features.
- **Large expressive display type** with tight tracking; variable-font weight shifts.
- **Product-in-context hero** (browser + phone mockups), not abstract 3D blobs.
- **Subtle scroll-triggered motion** and **count-up stats** — restrained, never
  jittery.
- **Generous whitespace**, soft shadows, gently rounded corners (matching the app's
  shadcn/ui feel).
- **Light theme primary**, with **one deliberate dark, accent-green section** (the
  final CTA) for rhythm and contrast.
- **Sticky CTA** and a slim, condensing nav.
- **Soft gradient-mesh glow** used once or twice as accent, not wallpaper.
- **Light/dark aware** and **accessible** (WCAG contrast, keyboard-navigable).

## 7. Voice & tone

Confident, plain-spoken, and local — the way a sharp Kenyan founder talks, not
corporate SaaS jargon. Short sentences. Verbs. Speak to *one freelancer*, not
"businesses." CTAs are action + reward ("Start free," "Send your first invoice").
A light touch of Kenyan pride is on-brand; keep it warm, not gimmicky.

## 8. Do / Don't

**Do:** show the real product · lead with M-Pesa + Kenya · repeat one CTA · say "no
card" · design mobile-first and fast · make the free tier feel generous.

**Don't:** use fake logos/testimonials · bury the CTA · drown it in gradient blobs or
meaningless 3D · use the default purple SaaS palette · write vague "empower your
business" filler · make it heavy/slow.

## 9. Brand assets (use the existing identity)

Canja already has a finished logo kit — **do not design a new logo.** Build the page
around it.

**What exists:**

- A **wordmark** ("Canja") in three treatments: **full-color**, **white** (for dark
  backgrounds), and **black/grayscale**.
- A standalone **icon mark** (the Canja symbol) in **color**, **white**, and
  **black**.

**How to use them:**

- **Nav:** full-color wordmark on the light canvas; switch to the **white wordmark**
  on the dark, accent-green final-CTA band and footer.
- **Favicon / compact spots / mobile app-bar:** the **icon mark**.
- **Hero product mockups:** the invoices and dashboard shown should carry the Canja
  wordmark as the sample org's branding — so the reader sees the product *and* the
  brand at once.
- **Palette:** anchor on Canja's **deep green `#103B05`** (already the brand color),
  warm off-white canvas, near-black ink, one bright accent for buttons. Pull any
  secondary tones from the color wordmark so the page and the logo feel like one
  system.
- **Consistency:** match the logo's geometry and weight in the display type and icon
  stroke, so the wordmark doesn't look bolted on.

## 10. Color palette (deep green + warm gold)

The anchor `#103B05` is a *deep brand* color — too dark to carry a light page on its
own. So the palette pairs a green **scale** (identity) with a **warm gold accent**
(CTAs that pop). Green reads *money, growth, trust*; gold against it reads
*energetic and premium* — the classic fintech pairing — and gives buttons the punch
an all-green page never gets. Gold is used **only** for actions and one or two
highlights; if everything is gold, nothing is.

**Core brand green** (scaled from `#103B05`)

| Role | Hex | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Green 900 | `#0B2A03` | Deepest — dark section / footer backgrounds |
| **Green 800 (brand)** | **`#103B05`** | Wordmark, dark CTA band, heading accents |
| Green 600 | `#1F6A16` | Text links, icon fills, secondary accents on light |
| Green 300 | `#8FD07E` | Illustrative fills, chart tints |
| Green 100 | `#E6F3E1` | Soft washes, hover backgrounds, badges |

**Accent / CTA — warm gold**

| Role | Hex | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Accent | `#F4A423` | **Primary buttons**, key highlights, underlines |
| Accent hover | `#DB9016` | Button hover / pressed |
| Accent soft | `#FDEFD4` | Highlight washes, "Pro" badges |

> **Button rule:** gold button with a **dark near-black label** (`#14231A`), *not
> white* — white on gold fails contrast; dark text on gold is punchy and accessible.

**Neutrals** (warm, so they sit with the green)

| Role | Hex | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Canvas | `#FBFAF7` | Page background (warm off-white, not stark white) |
| Surface | `#FFFFFF` | Cards, elevated blocks |
| Alt surface | `#F4F5F1` | Alternating section bands |
| Border | `#E7E5DE` | Hairlines, dividers |
| Ink | `#14231A` | Headings & body (near-black, green undertone) |
| Muted | `#6B7A70` | Secondary / subhead text |

**Dark band** (final CTA + footer)

| Role | Hex |
|---|---|
| Background | `#0B2A03` |
| Text | `#F3F9F0` |
| Muted text | `#A9C4A0` |
| Button | gold `#F4A423` (pops hardest here) with `#14231A` label |

**Semantic accents** — match the app, use sparingly for status / trust cues:
success `#2E7D32` · overdue/warning `#D97706` · danger `#DC2626` · info `#2F6DB5`.

**How it maps to the page:** warm canvas `#FBFAF7` for most sections, dark ink,
green links, one gold CTA; alternate `#FBFAF7` and `#F4F5F1` bands for rhythm; make
the **final CTA band + footer** the single deep-green `#0B2A03` moment (white
wordmark, gold button) — that contrast is what makes the page feel designed.

**Accessibility:** ink on canvas is ~15:1 (excellent); deep-green bands with
off-white text are very high contrast; the gold-with-dark-text button passes AA/AAA.
Keep body text on `#14231A` / `#6B7A70` — never gold or light green on white.

## 11. Reference screenshots (design direction)

**Attached to this brief are multiple screenshots that define the visual direction
we want to follow.** Treat them as the north-star for the *look and feel* — layout
patterns, section rhythm, type scale, use of whitespace, motion cues, and the
overall level of polish. Study them before designing.

How to reconcile them with everything above:

- **Screenshots drive the *look*; this brief drives the *strategy, content, and
  conversion structure*.** Adopt their layout and craft, but keep our section
  anatomy (§4), our one promise (§2), and our CTA discipline (§3).
- **Canja's brand always wins over the references' branding.** Use *our* logo kit
  (§9) and *our* palette (§10) — deep green + warm gold — even if the reference
  screenshots use other colors, fonts, or logos. Borrow their structure, not their
  identity.
- **Localize what you borrow.** Where a reference shows generic SaaS content, swap in
  our real substance: M-Pesa payments, KES, Kenyan context, actual Canja product UI.

If a reference and this brief ever conflict, the order of authority is:
**(1) Canja brand assets & palette → (2) this brief's strategy → (3) the reference
screenshots' visual style.**

## 12. Sitemap & information architecture

**Philosophy — hub and spoke.** The **Home page does ~80% of the selling in one
scroll**: a casual visitor should be able to understand Canja and sign up without
clicking anything. The supporting pages exist for the minority who want to go deeper
before committing, and — just as important — to capture search traffic so this
information actually gets *found*. So the goal ("the landing page is where users get
as much information about our product") is met by a **rich, comprehensive Home page
backed by focused deep-dive pages**, not by cramming everything onto one screen or
scattering the basics across many clicks.

**Primary navigation (top bar):** Home · Features · Pricing · Why Canja · Resources ·
**Log in** · **Start free** (the last two right-aligned; "Start free" is the solid
gold button).

**Sitemap**

- **Home** (`/`) — the master landing page (the full §4 anatomy). The hub everything
  points back to.
- **Features** — the product in depth.
  - Overview (`/features`) — all capabilities in one rich, scannable page.
  - Deep-dives (individual pages, great for SEO):
    - Invoicing (`/features/invoicing`)
    - Quotes & estimates (`/features/quotes`)
    - Payments & M-Pesa (`/features/payments`)
    - Reminders & follow-ups (`/features/reminders`)
    - Dashboard & reports (`/features/reports`)
    - Credit notes (`/features/credit-notes`)
    - Branding & templates (`/features/branding`)
- **How it works** (`/how-it-works`) — the Create → Send → Get paid flow expanded,
  with a short demo/walkthrough.
- **Who it's for** — segmented pages (they convert better and rank for intent):
  - Freelancers (`/for/freelancers`)
  - Small teams & agencies (`/for/agencies`)
- **Pricing** (`/pricing`) — dedicated Free vs Pro comparison, full feature matrix,
  pricing FAQ. Reinforce "free forever core · no card."
- **Why Canja** — the differentiation & comparison hub (SEO-critical, since Zoho is
  the anchor competitor):
  - Canja vs Zoho Invoice (`/compare/zoho`)
  - Canja vs spreadsheets (`/compare/spreadsheets`)
- **Resources** (`/resources` or `/blog`) — getting-paid-faster tips, M-Pesa for
  business, KRA/tax basics for freelancers. The SEO + trust engine.
- **Help / Support** (`/help`) — FAQ, getting-started guides, contact routes.
- **About** (`/about`) — story, mission, "Made in Kenya," the people/credibility
  behind a tool that handles money.
- **Contact** (`/contact`) — form, email, and a **WhatsApp** route (how Kenyans
  actually reach out).
- **Legal**
  - Privacy Policy (`/privacy`) — reference the Kenya Data Protection Act, 2019.
  - Terms of Service (`/terms`).
- **App entry points** (live in the product, linked from every marketing page):
  - Start free / Sign up (`/signup`)
  - Log in (`/login`)

**Footer (organizes the whole site):**

| Product | Company | Resources | Legal |
|---|---|---|---|
| Features | About | Blog / Resources | Privacy |
| Pricing | Contact | Help / FAQ | Terms |
| How it works | Careers* | M-Pesa guide | Data protection |
| For freelancers | | Compare | |
| For agencies | | | |

*Plus: social links, "Made in Kenya 🇰🇪", and a final **Start free** button. (`*` =
optional / when relevant.)

**Conversion paths.** Every page carries the **Start free** CTA in the nav and at
least once in-body; deep-dive and comparison pages end with a contextual CTA back to
Home or straight to signup. No page is a dead end.

**Build order (MVP first).** Ship **Home + Pricing + a Privacy/Terms pair + the
signup/login links** first — that is a complete, launch-ready site. Then add **Why
Canja / vs Zoho** and the **Resources/blog** (the SEO engine), then the feature
deep-dives and use-case pages as content is ready. Don't block launch on the full
tree.
